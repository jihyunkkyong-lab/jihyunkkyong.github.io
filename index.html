About

I study what happens when AI enters spaces built for humans — knowledge communities, online fundraising, creative markets — and how design and language shape the way people respond.

I am a master's student in Business and Technology Management at KAIST, working on human–AI interaction and the socio-economic consequences of generative AI on digital platforms. My approach pairs large-scale analysis of user behavior with causal inference and natural language processing, turning dimensions once treated as qualitative into measurable evidence.

Research

Working Papers

Why Do Users Dislike AI-Generated Answers? Evidence from a Social Q&A Platform

In progress

Advised by Prof. Chulho Lee, KAIST

  • Q&A records
  • NLP
  • Crawled and compiled 240,000+ Q&A records from a major social Q&A platform, enabling large-scale empirical analysis of user responses to AI-generated content.
  • Used NLP to parameterize linguistic and stylistic features of AI-generated text, quantifying dimensions previously treated as qualitative.
  • Identified systematic patterns of user aversion toward AI-generated answers, adding causal evidence to the discourse on human–AI interaction in knowledge communities.

In progress

Co-authored with Prof. Jooyoung Park, Peking University

  • GoFundMe
  • Panel data
  • Exploiting the staggered rollout of AI-assisted fundraising features on GoFundMe as a natural experiment to estimate their effects on fundraising outcomes and the linguistic diversity of campaign narratives.
  • Assembling a campaign-level panel dataset to support a natural experiment design around the feature launch.
